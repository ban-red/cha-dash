# Changelog

Everything that changes in Cha Dash, written for the people who run it.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Until 1.0, a new minor version (0.2, 0.3, …) may change the config, the API or how the image behaves; its notes will always say what to do. Each release's section here is also the text of its [GitHub release](https://github.com/ban-red/cha-dash/releases), so links in it are full URLs.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **Arrange the Cameras tile.** In Widget settings, tick cameras and move them up or down to set their order, and give each a size: one cell, two wide, two tall or 2 × 2. The tile fits them in that order and says how many more didn't fit. On Auto, the first camera is the big one, as before.
- **House power is off by default on the Home Assistant tile.** Turn on **Show house power** in Widget settings to bring back the power graph and energy today. Without it, wide and large tiles show cards for what your house reports: Indoors (thermostat temperature and what it's doing), Safety (smoke, CO, gas and leak sensors: OK, or what tripped) and Playing (media players, with the track). The home stat names who is home. A tripped safety sensor marks the tile and heads Needs a look. The small tile shows who's home with lights and switches, and indoors temperature at the foot.

## [0.2.0-beta.2] - 2026-10-29

The second beta of v0.2: **Cha Dash remembers, and tiles say more.** Metrics and health now keep a history on disk, so sparklines survive a restart, a sheet looks back over what was kept, and the Pulse strip under the Briefing takes the whole board back to any moment of the last 24 hours. Tiles get views you swipe between, new sizes, a menu, and far more from Plex, Tautulli, qBittorrent, Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr, Home Assistant, Frigate and the weather. Settings → Bring in imports Homarr 2 backups and plain YAML boards. The v0.2 actions have now run against a real homelab, all but Pi-hole's, AdGuard Home's and Seerr's, and what those runs and a second security review found is fixed below.

**History is on by default.** It writes `history.db` next to the database, tens of MB at a thousand series, and reads what your boards show once a minute. Set `CHADASH_HISTORY=off` to keep nothing, or `CHADASH_HISTORY_DIR` to put the file elsewhere. Nothing else needs changing from 0.2.0-beta.1. As before, point both images at `beta`, as the [install steps](https://github.com/ban-red/cha-dash/blob/main/deploy/README.md) show, or pin `0.2.0-beta.2`.

- **Section settings.** The section header's menu has a Section settings item to rename the section. An empty name clears the title.
- **Add a section next to another.** The section header's menu has Add new section before and after, which insert an empty, full-width "New section" beside it.
- **Remove a section from its menu.** The section header's menu has a new Remove section item. It asks first, then removes the section and every widget in it, with Undo.
- **Camera pictures and controls for Frigate.** Turn on **Show camera pictures** in a Frigate connection and the Cameras tile shows each camera's current picture instead of a drawing. Cha Dash fetches it from Frigate (`latest.jpg`, nothing else) and passes it on, refreshing only while the tile is on screen; everyone signed in to Cha Dash can then see the pictures. There's no live video. Add an admin **control account** to the connection and Cha Dash can turn a camera's detection, recording, snapshots and alerts on and off, or the whole camera (Frigate 0.16 or later), each with Undo. Cameras now have a sheet: click a frame for a larger picture, the camera's switches and its last detections, with the controls under Actions. The Panel, Large and Hero tiles have a control row for the main camera.
- **Widget settings of their own.** A widget can now offer its own settings under Widget settings. The Cameras tile is the first: which cameras it shows, pictures on or off, how often they refresh, the detections list and the controls.
- **A clearer Up next tile.** Each release has a mark for its kind (episode, film or album), and premieres and finales get a label. Wide shows the next release large, with how long until it, beside the rest of today and tomorrow. Panel adds the week as a ribbon, a dot per release. Tall and Column mark the next release and draw a line at now in today's list; what aired in the last hour is dimmed. Large lists the week a row per day, under each date.
- **A fuller Weather tile.** Three new sizes. Wide draws the next six hours as a temperature curve beside now. Panel (4×3) adds the sun's arc from sunrise to sunset, with where it is now, and feels like, wind and its direction, humidity and the chance of rain. Tall (2×4) lists feels like, wind and rain over five days shown as bars on the week's range. Large (4×4) has all of it. Partly cloudy gets its own mark, sun or moon behind a cloud. It uses the forecast Cha Dash already reads, so there are no new requests.
- **The Home Assistant tile shows what needs a look.** Under the stats: how many doors and windows are open, locks unlocked, batteries low and updates waiting, and the alarm's state. Small and wide tiles page to a **Needs a look** list (which door, which battery and its level, which update from which version); the 4×4 tile shows it in place, with how many entities are unavailable. A triggered alarm marks the tile. It all comes from the states Cha Dash already reads, so there are no new requests.
- **Neon and Neon Tokyo themes.** A night-city HUD look with square corners and mono headlines. Neon is blue-black with a signal-yellow accent, orange for attention, hot red for down and cyan for maintenance. Neon Tokyo is indigo with a hot-pink accent, amber for attention, red for down and cyan for maintenance. Both pass the same contrast checks as the other themes. Pick them in Settings or ⌘K.
- **The Now playing tile shows what's on Plex.** With Plex alone (no Tautulli needed) it lists the titles playing, with who's watching, on which player, how far along, and whether it plays directly or transcodes. Under the count: how many streams transcode and the bandwidth they use. Small and wide tiles page to **Recently added** (the newest movies, seasons and albums); the 4×4 tile shows both with the last 24 hours, and how many streams are remote. The library line adds episodes and music artists. Cha Dash now reads Plex's recently added list every 5 minutes while you watch, and counts episodes and artists with the other library counts.
- **Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr tiles show what's queued and what's wanted.** Small and wide tiles page to a Queue list (each download with its progress, time left, client, and why one is stuck) and a Wanted list (what's missing, with its date), both scrolling. The integrations read the first 25 of each.
- **Hide a warning until it turns critical.** A warning on Needs attention has a Hide button (the Inbox has Hide until critical), with Undo. It leaves Needs attention and the briefing's count, and comes back as soon as it turns critical. It's remembered on this device only. Critical problems, repairs and approvals can't be hidden.
- **The qBittorrent tile shows the torrents.** It lists what's downloading (progress, speed, time left) and what's uploading, with the global ratio, free space and peers underneath, instead of only the other apps' queues. qBittorrent now reports up to 8 active torrents, their names included. The tile falls back to the queue rows when there are none. A second view, Torrents, is a compact table of the torrents (up to 200) with their status, a filter and a sort in a small bar, and it scrolls. Newest first by default. The qBittorrent instance has a **Torrents listed** setting (0–1000, default 200) for how many it sends, and the Downloads tile now offers a 4×4 size.
- **Two more tile sizes: Panel (4×3) and Column (3×4).** Panel gives a list 5–6 rows under a headline; Column is a taller list that's wider than Tall. Fleet, Guests, Containers, Unraid array, Unraid server, Up next, Incidents, Downloads and Bookmarks offer both; Host offers Panel. Column is 3 wide, so on a 4-column grid it leaves one column beside it.
- **Tautulli shows what's playing and what was watched.** The tile lists the titles playing now, with who's watching, how far along and whether it's transcoding, and has a second view with the last few things watched. Cha Dash reads two more things from Tautulli: the sessions it already fetched, and `get_history` (optional, 8 rows).
- **Leave layout editing with Escape, or a button.** Escape exits edit mode, and an Exit layout editing button stays at the foot of the page while you edit.
- **Reorder from a tile's menu.** Move a widget earlier or later in its section, or to its start or end.
- **A menu on section headers.** Hover a section's title for a three-dot menu with Resize, to set the section's width from a quarter of the board to the full row, and Reorder, to move it up or down.
- **Swipe a tile for more.** Some tiles now have more than one view. Swipe sideways on a phone or tablet, or click the dots at the bottom of the tile. A small weather tile has the next hours; a host tile has its guests or containers and its storage; a Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr tile has what's coming next, and a small service tile its last 24 hours. Each tile remembers its view on that device. Tiles with nothing more to show look as before.
- **A menu on every tile.** The settings cog on a tile is now three dots that open a compact menu with icons: Integration settings, Widget settings, Resize (the sizes that fit its section) and Remove widget, which asks first and offers Undo.
- **An Unraid server widget.** One tile for the whole box: CPU, memory and array fill as bars, then parity, the hottest disk, uptime, containers, VMs and shares, in six sizes. A stopped array or a disabled disk marks it down; invalid or overdue parity, a hot or full disk, or containers that need a look mark it for attention. Add it from the picker under Servers once Unraid is connected.
- **Settings and integration setup, tidied.** The Settings menu is in three groups: Connect, Keep, and People & access. Each pane says what it is for under its title. The integrations list puts anything needing repair first and gets a filter once you have seven or more. The catalog opens with the filter focused, Enter opens the best match, and it marks what you already have instead of showing tier and version. Adding an integration shows three steps (credential, details, test and save); Test is the main button until it passes, and Save and Test stay in view on a long form. On a narrow screen the recipe comes first, with a link to skip to the form. The Add list is grouped (Media, Media automation, Downloads, Hosts & backups, Network, Monitoring, Home, Files & notes), and its filter also matches other names such as Overseerr. Settings → API tokens, Notifications, Users, Nodes and Stacks fold their long explainers behind a one-line summary; Audit lists events first and the chain check below.
- **Metrics history on disk.** Cha Dash now keeps what integrations report in `history.db`, next to the database: 1-minute points for 48 hours and 15-minute points for 14 days, each with the average, low and high. Sparklines no longer go blank for a day after a restart, and a finished half hour shows its average rather than one sample. It's on by default and takes tens of MB at a thousand series. `CHADASH_HISTORY=long-term` also keeps hourly points for a year and daily points forever; `CHADASH_HISTORY=off` keeps nothing. `CHADASH_HISTORY_DIR` moves the file, for example off an SD card.
- **Look back on a container's sheet.** The Metrics tab has Now, 1 h, 24 h, 7 d and 30 d. The ranges show the averages Cha Dash kept, with the peak. Containers are read only while their sheet is open, so expect gaps. Scripts can read the same with `GET /api/history` and a `graph.read` token.
- **Health history and uptime.** Cha Dash records every status change. A sheet's History tab shows the last week as a bar, the uptime while Cha Dash was watching, and the latest changes. "Down since" no longer resets when Cha Dash restarts, and the time Cha Dash was stopped isn't counted as up. `GET /api/history/states` gives the same to scripts.
- **Settings → History.** Choose Standard, Long-term or Off, and it applies at once, no restart. Each level says about how big it grows for your hub. The pane shows what's on disk and can clear it all. A size limit, 1 GB by default, keeps `history.db` in check: past it, the oldest points go first, and health history is kept. Each integration can be left out (its health is still recorded) or have its history cleared on its own.
- **Prometheus `/metrics`.** Set `CHADASH_METRICS_LISTEN` (say `127.0.0.1:3697`) and Prometheus or VictoriaMetrics can scrape every series' current value, each resource's health and the hub's own memory, for keeping as long as you like. It's off by default, on a listener of its own, and `CHADASH_METRICS_TOKEN` adds a bearer token. `CHADASH_HISTORY=off` still turns history off altogether.
- **Choose how long each level of detail is kept.** Settings → History has a select per row: by the minute from 6 hours to 14 days, by the quarter hour from 2 days to a year, and the long-term ones up to 10 years or forever. The estimates follow. Shorter retention applies within a minute. A coarser level can't be kept for less time than a finer one, so long ranges never go blank.
- **Leave single metrics out of history.** Settings → History → By metric takes patterns such as `docker:*#net_rx`, where `*` stands for anything, and says how many series each matches. Health is still recorded.
- **Download CSV from a sheet.** On a Metrics tab's 1 h to 30 d ranges, Download CSV saves the averages, lows and highs shown, one row a point, with UTC times.
- **The Pulse strip.** Under the Briefing, a band shows the whole home's health over the last 24 hours, by the quarter hour. Drag across it, or focus it and use the arrow keys, and the board travels back: tiles show their health at that moment, every sparkline marks its value, and the strip names what wasn't well. `GET /api/history/pulse` gives the same to scripts.
- **Bursts show on rate graphs.** A sparkline point is a half hour's average, so a few minutes at full speed used to vanish. The WAN and download speed graphs now shade each half hour's peak above the line, and hovering names it ("20 Mb/s 6 h ago · peak 940 Mb/s"). The newest point follows the current half hour's average instead of sticking at its first reading.
- **Linked scrubbing.** Pressing and dragging on one sparkline on the board marks the same moment on all the others. Hovering reads that graph alone.
- **History without a tab open.** While Cha Dash keeps history, it reads what a board shows once a minute even when nobody's looking, instead of every 5 minutes, so overnight charts have no gaps. That's a little more load on your services; setting history to Off goes back to the idle rate. Container stats are still read only while their sheet is open.
- **More Unraid control.** A controllable Unraid connection can now restart, pause and unpause containers, pause, resume and reboot VMs, force-stop a VM, and remove a stopped container. Removing needs a key with Docker delete access, from the new "full control" consent link in the Unraid recipe; the rest need only the update access Control already asks for. The names come from Unraid's published API and are tested against a fake server, so try them on a throwaway container and VM first. A new **Also let Cha Dash run parity checks** switch on the connection adds start, pause, resume and cancel for the array's parity check. It needs a key with Array update access, from a new consent link in the recipe.
- **Turn a check into its own integration.** On a checks connection, Settings now lists the targets that look like an app Cha Dash has an integration for ("Jupiter Unraid", "Linkding"). "Set up" opens the add form with the name and address filled in, so only the credential is left.
- **Mini graphs, redesigned.** Trends on tiles are a smooth curve over a soft gradient with a fading band for the normal range and a haloed end dot. Hover or touch one to see the value and when. Before there is history, it is a dotted line instead of an empty grey block.
- **Service logos on tiles.** A tile bound to an integration, or just named after a service ("Plex Media Server", "Uptime Kuma"), shows that service's logo (Sonarr, Proxmox and about a hundred more) when the hub has no icon cached for it. The logos are bundled; they come from the Apache-2.0 dashboard-icons project.
- **Settings → Bring in** replaces Settings → Import. It has three tabs, Import, Discover and History, and everything goes through one preview before anything changes.
- **Drop a config anywhere on the page.** Cha Dash names its source from the files: Homepage's `services.yaml`, `docker.yaml` and `settings.yaml`, a Homarr backup, or a board written in YAML. A Cha Dash export opens Backup instead.
- **The Review sheet.** Every connection and tile is a row you can untick. Leaving a connection out leaves out the tiles that need it. Add missing keys right there, reuse a connection you already have, and pick where tiles go: a new board, an existing board, or connections only. A picture of the board shows what you'll get. Apply writes exactly what you reviewed; if something changed in between, it shows the new plan instead.
- **Import from Homarr 2**, from the backup zip (Management → Tools → Backup). Homarr keeps integration secrets encrypted with its own key, so each connection asks for its key again. Homarr 0.x configs aren't imported; upgrade to Homarr 2 first.
- **Plain YAML boards** (`kind: Board`): sections, tiles in order, sizes, and the services they connect to, without IDs or coordinates. The format is in `internal/importer/yamlboard/README.md`.
- **Discover, any time.** Cha Dash suggests services it already sees through what's connected, like a Sonarr container or a Traefik route to `sonarr.…`, without probing the network to find them. Check sends one request to each address shown. Picked ones go through the same preview. Dismiss is remembered for the whole hub, with Undo. New ones appear as one item in the Inbox.
- **Import history, with Revert.** Every import is listed with what it made. Revert shows its plan first: it removes only what the import created and nobody changed since, keeps the rest and says why, and asks you to confirm it's you before removing connections.
- The first-run wizard's Import step now runs inside the wizard, with the same preview. An empty board offers the ways in.

- **A settings cog on every tile.** Hover or focus a tile and a cog appears in its top-right corner. It opens the settings of the integration behind the tile, or the tile's Edit when no integration feeds it (the clock, say). Viewers who can do neither don't see it.

- **Check now.** A failing check's problem in the Inbox has a Check now button that runs the instance's checks at once, instead of waiting for the next interval (60 s by default). Behind it is `POST /api/integrations/instances/{id}/refresh`, open to viewers and up.

- **Test connection checks the integration again.** Testing a saved integration also runs its health check, so a fixed connection clears its status and its problem in the Inbox right away.

- **Test one check target.** Each target in the Checks form has its own Test button, which checks just that target and says what it found, without saving.

- **Warning icons explain themselves.** Hover over a tile's warning icon, or tab to it, to see the warning; click it to read it in full.
- **Long tile notes open in full.** A warning longer than two lines, such as Prowlarr's own health messages, is cut with "more" on the tile. Click it to read the whole message in a dialog.
- **Bento and Mosaic boards.** Edit layout has a Layout choice: **Sections** (side by side in rows, as before), **Bento** (sections fit together, so a short section no longer leaves a hole beside a tall one; a section is only as wide as its tiles, and narrows to fill a gap it fits) and **Mosaic** (every tile fits together, with each section's name on its first tile). It's saved with the board for everyone. Nothing reshuffles: the same board always looks the same, and tile sizes don't change. A Mosaic board is edited as Bento.
- **Bazarr says what its health issues are.** The tile lists them as Bazarr does ("/tv: Directory does not exist") instead of only counting them. The note links to Bazarr's System → Status page, or System → Providers for throttled providers, and opens it in a new tab; a cut note's dialog has the same link.

### Changed

- **Search for missing runs only for fewer than 10.** Sonarr, Radarr and Lidarr's Search for missing is offered while fewer than 10 monitored items are missing. With more, a search for all of them can hammer your indexers, so the resource sheet and ⌘K offer **Open Missing in …** instead, which opens the app's Missing page to pick from.
- **Wide tiles use their height.** On a wide (4x2) tile the trend graph now grows to fill the column instead of staying a thin strip, and Tautulli lists what's playing there.
- **A new widget picker.** Search the catalog, by name or by what a widget can show ("cpu" finds Metric), or browse it by group. Each widget is a card with what it shows and its sizes. The one you pick appears live, with your data, at the size you choose and in proportion, before it goes on the board. Long lists of what a widget can show (64 metrics) have their own filter. Arrow keys move through the cards, ⌘↵ or a double-click adds.
- **Homepage import recognises apps with no widget.** A service whose icon or name is Unraid, Linkding, Plex, Sonarr or any other app Cha Dash has an integration for now gets that integration, ready for its API key, instead of only a check in the shared "Homepage" checks instance. It keeps the check for its status. Import again from Settings → Bring in to pick this up; the old import can be reverted in History.
- **A tile bound to an integration is named after it.** Rename the integration in Settings and its tile follows at once. In the tile editor the Name field is greyed out for these tiles.
- **Edit layout is an icon in the top-right button row**, beside search and Settings. It toggles edit mode; the text button above the board is gone.
- **Checks say Up or Down.** A check's tile leads with Up or Down instead of an HTTP status code; the code, latency and uptime sit below it.
- **A tile's address opens the service.** The address at the bottom of a check or service tile is a link that opens in a new tab.
- **Long units wrap on small tiles.** A unit too long to sit beside its number ("subtitles missing") moves to the line below instead of running off the tile.
- **A tile's status mark sits bottom right** when it stands alone, clear of the settings cog. With words beside it ("WAN down since 14:02") it stays with them.
- **The certificate fingerprint field shows only for pin and tofu,** the two modes that use it.
- **Optional groups are folded.** A group of optional settings, such as TLS, starts closed, with what it holds in its header ("verify", "pin · 3f2a1b9c…"). It opens by itself when a field inside needs fixing.
- **Compact forms.** Inputs, selects and choice rows across the app are shorter and tighter, with number fields side by side. On touch screens they keep their full size.
- **A check target shows only its own fields:** URL, expected status and TLS for http; host and port for tcp. Switching a target's kind drops the fields that no longer apply when you save.
- **A settings button in the top right** replaces the theme button. Its menu has Theme, Settings and Sign out; Theme opens the theme previews in the same menu, and Escape steps back.

### Fixed

- **A link tile with a warning says what's wrong.** A small service tile showed a warning icon with no words, because small tiles hide the header's text. The reason now shows in the tile.
- **Docker through the node after a hub restart.** If the hub started before its node reconnected, the Docker instance showed "invalid config" for up to five minutes and its actions were unavailable. It now retries within seconds.
- **Remove with volumes deletes the anonymous volumes.** After Cha Dash had recreated a container, removing it with its volumes kept the anonymous volume while the task said it was deleted. The node now deletes them itself and reports what it kept.
- **Secret fields have one input.** Every secret in an integration's form showed a second, empty text box under it. What you typed there was never saved.
- **A self-signed certificate is a warning on a check, not an outage.** An HTTP check against a LAN service with an untrusted certificate showed it as down and raised a problem. It now shows as up with a warning naming the fingerprint to pin. A pin mismatch still fails.
- **"Review the check" opens the check's settings.** It and other "open the integration" suggestions failed with "There's no action called integration.settings" outside the Demo Home.
- **Long errors wrap.** A certificate fingerprint or long error in the Inbox ran past its card or was cut off. Long facts now sit under their label, and hashes and URLs break to fit.
- **Proxmox tiles show the node.** An imported Proxmox tile was empty apart from its link. It now shows the node's CPU, memory and guests running, or for a cluster the nodes online and guests running, and turns red or amber with its worst node.
- **A tile shows its own integration's trouble.** A Plex tile whose token was rejected before Plex was ever read showed Tautulli instead, as idle. Tiles bound to an integration (all imported ones) now say it needs repair and offer Reconnect, for Plex, the *arrs, Home Assistant, Frigate, OPNsense, Traefik, Uptime Kuma and downloads alike.
- **Section headers line up.** A section header without a note beside its name sat a few pixels higher than its neighbours, and so did its tiles.
- **Spaces between words are wider.** Numbers ran into the word after them, as in "1services".
- **Cha Dash knows the Proxmox guest it runs in, behind a Docker bridge.** Its Docker engine, reached through the node, was only suggested as running on the guest with its name. With the guest's cores and memory too, the link is made, so shutting down, stopping or rebooting Cha Dash's own guest is T4 (off by default) instead of an ordinary action. Setting `hub_vmid` is still the sure way.
- **A reverse proxy's 404 isn't "the API changed".** When a service behind a proxy was down, the proxy's 404 raised an API-changed repair. A 404 before anything else answered now reads as unreachable.
- **Cha Dash's own stack is protected with an override file.** With a `compose.override.yaml`, Settings → Stacks listed it as "can't adopt" and accepted drafts under its name; deploying one was still refused. It's now shown as Cha Dash's own stack, and drafts are refused.
- A deploy that rolls back on its own now ends its log on the rollback's commit, not the attempt's.
- **A protected container says why it can't be removed.** Removing a running container the node protects asked you to stop it first, which the node also refused. It now says the node protects it, with the container's name as written.
- **A half-filled control credential points at what's missing.** With only a Control token ID filled in, Test connection and Save showed the hub's error while the secret's field stayed folded away. They now open the control section at the empty field. The first-run wizard also names chadash-node, not the socket proxy, when that's how it reaches Docker.
- **A guest that was running is running after a rollback.** Rolling a Proxmox guest back to a snapshot without RAM left it stopped. Cha Dash now asks Proxmox VE to start it again, and the plan says so.
- **Stacks on a node that doesn't exist are refused.** Saving a draft under a node's name instead of its ID kept it, for a node the hub doesn't know. It's now refused. An offline node is named in the error, and Cha Dash's own stack, when it isn't one file the node can read, says only its containers are shown.
- **chadash-node logs the policy it loaded.** With a policy file mounted, the node said nothing about it. It now logs what the file grants, denies and protects at start.
- **Guests outside the control token's reach can't be planned.** A Proxmox guest outside the control token's pool, or one its role lacks a privilege on, planned like any other and failed with a 403 once approved. Its actions are now unavailable, naming the privilege and the guest.
- **A rollback leaves untouched services alone.** Rolling a stack back, on its own or when asked, pinned every service to its image digest, so it recreated services the failed deploy never changed, and their pins stayed in the file. Only the services it recreates are pinned now.
- **Home Assistant scripts show up to run.** With scripts on the instance's allow-list, Run a script was never offered on the sheet or in ⌘K, though it could be planned through the API. Each allowed script that can run now is offered on its own, as Run script.… in Home Assistant.
- **Undoing a qBittorrent pause resumes only what it paused.** Undo used to resume every torrent, so torrents you had paused yourself started too. Pause and resume now remember which torrents they changed, and their undo touches only those.
- **A saved secret can be removed in the integration form.** An optional secret Cha Dash holds, such as a control token under "Let Cha Dash act", has a Remove button; Keep it undoes that until you save, and Save deletes it from the vault. Before, the form could only replace a secret, so the only way to stop Cha Dash acting was to delete the whole integration.
- **A PBS prune the control token can't do is refused when planned.** With a control token weaker than DatastoreAdmin, Prune per policy planned and then failed once approved, saying the datastore had no prune job. Planning now checks the token's rights on the datastore and names what it lacks, and a job that disappears before the run says so.
- **Accessibility fixes from the review.** The widget picker no longer reads its whole detail pane aloud on every arrow key, its faint text on the selected card and rows is readable (4.5:1), a widget not available here keeps its text at full contrast, the link error is tied to its field, and Add says why it can't go yet. The resource sheet keeps Tab inside it. Nodes → Revoke moves focus into its confirm and back. A warning glyph's target is 24 px, and Esc closes its tooltip wherever focus is. A field Tab reaches no longer hides under the floating Edit layout row or a form's Save bar. The demo clock no longer says UTC to screen readers when it shows the hub's zone.

### Security

- **Signing in to your own account no longer resets the guesses at someone else's.** The five-wrong-passwords ban counted per address, but any good sign-in from that address cleared it, so a household member could guess at the owner's password between their own sign-ins. A good sign-in now clears only the wrong passwords typed for that same username. Many addresses failing once each also no longer wipe another address's count.
- **Creating the owner and dev login refuse names a web page could rebind.** Until the owner exists, anyone who reaches the hub may create it, and a page on someone else's domain could point its name at the hub's address and do it from a browser on your LAN. Setup now answers only at an IP address, a local name (`hub`, `hub.lan`, `hub.local`, `*.home.arpa` and the like), `CHADASH_PUBLIC_URL`'s host, or through a trusted proxy. Dev login answers only at `localhost` or a loopback address.
- **Check now can't hammer a service.** Asks for the same connection within 10 seconds of each other, or within a minute of a failed attempt, are dropped, so a loop of Check nows can't walk a bad credential into the service's own lockout. A Test that works still syncs at once. Household no longer sees a Check now button the hub refuses it.
- **Discover's Check stays off link-local addresses.** A lead can come from a container's labels, so Check now refuses link-local (where cloud metadata answers), unspecified and multicast addresses, as the icon and notification fetches already did.
- **History reads can't exhaust the hub's memory.** `GET /api/history` sized its answer by the range asked for, so `from=0` across 20 series could allocate gigabytes, and a date far in the future crashed the request. Any range now costs at most a full chart: the newest points of the coarsest kept level, up to now.
- **Imported files can't turn certificate checks off.** A YAML board's `tls` is left out, and listed as left out; TLS is chosen from the URL. Set up from a check target that skips certificate checks pins the certificate instead.

### Security

- **Open live connections now follow sign-outs and account changes.** Signing out, being disabled, a role change and a handover of ownership close that person's open tabs' live connections at once; the page reconnects as whoever it is now. Each connection also rechecks its session every minute (so an expired one ends) and reconnects after 12 hours.
- **Container logs are for operators and up.** Reading a container's logs (`docker.container.logs`) was open to viewers and household because it's T0; logs often hold secrets, so it now needs Operator or above. Household accounts run only actions marked for the household, at every tier. Log lines stay in the live task and are no longer saved with it, so they're gone after a restart.
- **More changes reach the audit log.** Integration connections (created, changed, deleted: names, the fields that changed and the URL's host, never a secret), passkeys added, renamed and removed, step-ups that worked and that failed, board layout saves and dismissed suggestions, "same thing?" decisions, the first-run wizard and its discovery, imports and exports (counts and IDs), and stack drafts. Refusals are recorded too: changing the owner's account, invalid users, a token scope your role can't hand out, revoking someone else's token. A change is recorded even when the browser hangs up mid-request.
- **Admins no longer see or revoke the owner's API tokens**, as they can't change the owner's account.
- **Two quick approvals can't share one T4 confirmation.** The step-up a critical action spends is now used up in the same step that starts it.
- **A token's actions name its user** in the audit log (`on_behalf_of`). Older entries keep their exact bytes and hashes.
- **Household accounts no longer get chadash-node's details or addresses** in the Home Graph (live, `GET /api/graph` and the first page load). Boards render as before.
- **Record the audit log's head.** `chadash audit verify`, `GET /api/audit/verify` and the audit export (`X-Chadash-Audit-Head`) now show the last event's number and hash. The chain can't protect itself from someone who can write the database file; keep the head somewhere else and check later exports still contain it (SECURITY.md).
- **A browser's push subscription can't be taken over** by another account: subscribing an endpoint someone else registered is refused. Turn notifications off in the first account to move a browser.
- **NAT64 and 6to4 addresses** are checked by the IPv4 address inside them, for Web Push and the icon fetcher.
- **SVG icons are parsed as XML and checked against an allowlist** of elements and attributes, so encoded `javascript:`, animation tricks and external references are refused.
- **Links are http(s) only**: a tile or widget link with any other scheme (`javascript:`, `data:`) renders without a link. A notification click can only open a page on the hub.
- **The icon fetcher has its own rules** (SECURITY.md): only the URLs your tiles and connections name, never loopback, link-local or cloud metadata addresses (checked after DNS), one redirect on the same host, short timeouts, 256 KiB, PNG, ICO, WebP or SVG only, and SVGs with scripts or external references refused. Icons are served with a sandbox CSP.
- **The node's pairing secret is single-use.** Pairing spends it and the hub writes a fresh one into the `pairing` volume, so a copy of it is worth nothing once the node has paired. The hub also writes a fresh one when a node is revoked and at start once a node has paired, and an admin can regenerate it (`POST /api/nodes/pairing/regenerate`, audited). Nothing to do: a fresh install still pairs by itself, and the node reads the new secret from the file when it needs it.
- **Pairing never replaces an enrolled node.** Pairing under the name of a live node is refused. To replace a node whose volume was lost, revoke the old one first. Pairing is accepted only from private and loopback addresses; `CHADASH_NODE_PAIRING_FROM` changes that.
- **`stacks.deploy` can't reach the node's own keys.** A stack can no longer bind the Docker socket, `/`, the node's directories or volumes, or the `pairing` volume, take another container's volumes, or read env files outside its directory; `include` and `extends` from other files are refused. Privileged containers, host namespaces, devices, `cap_add` and host paths still work, but the editor and the deploy confirmation now say they're root on that host, and the node's policy can deny them (`stacks.deny`, `stacks.bind_roots`). Deploying a stack is root on the node's host by design; deploy/README.md says how to lock it down.
- **Protected containers are protected from stacks too.** A deploy, plan or rollback that would recreate or remove a container `docker.protect` names is refused. The hub's container is protected whatever its service is called, and a node that couldn't tell its own compose project at start asks again.
- **Rollbacks send only the stack's own secrets.** A rollback sends only secrets an earlier deploy sent to that stack on that node; validation takes secret names from the hub's own reading of the draft. A node can't ask for another stack's secrets.
- **Smaller fixes.** A node can't announce another node's key (or the hub's) as its next one. Unauthenticated `/node` connections are cheaper to hold: the hello is read under 64 KiB, frames are buffered as they arrive, handshakes are capped per address and overall, unknown keys count against the rate limit, and their refusals log once a minute. Integrations reach only the node verbs they use, by name. Environment redaction covers `Config.Env` only; command lines and labels come back as they are (now documented).
- **Sign-in rate limits are harder to get around.** IPv6 clients are limited per /64 rather than per address. A flood of new addresses no longer resets everyone's budget. And each account allows 20 wrong passwords, then one a minute, wherever they come from; passkeys aren't affected.
- **Settings → Security warns about a reverse proxy Cha Dash doesn't trust**, and about a sign-in over plain HTTP when `CHADASH_PUBLIC_URL` is https. Both are logged once, too. Until the proxy is in `CHADASH_TRUSTED_PROXIES`, every sign-in shares one rate limit and the session cookie isn't marked Secure.
- **An instance with certificate checks off keeps saying so** in Settings → Integrations, not only when you test it.
- **The node channel is mutual TLS 1.3 inside the WebSocket**, each side known by a pinned Ed25519 key, with keepalive pings, size limits and version negotiation. Pairing checks the hub's key before the node sends its secret. Nodes can be revoked (`POST /api/nodes/{id}/revoke`), and keys rotate over the live channel.
- **Built with Go 1.27.3 and golang.org/x/net 0.61.** Together they fix 14 vulnerabilities in code Cha Dash uses: compromised certificate intermediates are distrusted, HTTP/1 and HTTP/2 connections can't be desynchronised or exhausted the ways now known, Range headers are bounded, and an `html/template` escaping bug is fixed.
- **The node writes its own files under the stacks root through `os.Root`**, so neither a `..` nor a symlink can take a write outside it.
- **The recipe for Docker on another host** explains that the proxy serves container inspects, environment variables included, to anything connecting from Cha Dash's address. It suggests publishing on one interface or using a VPN, and shows how to turn inspects off.

### Added

- **The front page.** Boards and sections you arrange by dragging, in place or in edit mode, with layout history and undo. A one-line briefing says how the home is doing, and a **Needs attention** section appears on its own when something does. The phone layout is worked out for you.
- **Widgets for the v0.1 catalog**, each designed for its sizes and its states (loading, empty, healthy, attention, down, stale, needs repair): service tile, fleet card, host card, guest list, container list, Unraid array, storage bar, camera hero, Home Assistant summary, media calendar, now playing, download queue, uptime check, metric stat and sparkline, bookmarks, clock and weather.
- **26 integrations, plus weather**, all read-only, each tested against recorded or documented API responses:
  - infrastructure: Proxmox VE (several instances and clusters), Proxmox Backup Server, Unraid (API 4.37.4 or later), Docker (through a read-only socket proxy; containers with Homepage's `homepage.*` labels are offered as tiles), Traefik, OPNsense, Uptime Kuma, and built-in HTTP and TCP checks;
  - home: Home Assistant and Frigate;
  - media and downloads: Plex (with Sign in with Plex), Jellyfin, Sonarr, Radarr, Prowlarr, qBittorrent, SABnzbd, Tautulli, Bazarr and Seerr;
  - network: Pi-hole (v6) and AdGuard Home;
  - apps: Immich, Mealie, Linkding and Karakeep;
  - weather from Open-Meteo, with no key and no account.
- **One picture of your home.** When several sources see the same thing (a container that both Docker and Unraid report), it shows once. Cha Dash links what runs where, what routes to what and what monitors what, explains a failed Proxmox backup by the backup server being down, and asks before merging anything it isn't sure about.
- **The Inbox.** Problems, repairs (an integration that needs a new key, with the fix), and discoveries, such as a labelled container that could become a tile, or two things that might be one.
- **⌘K** to search and open anything on the page.
- **Themes:** Instrument Dark and Light, OLED, Night, High Contrast and Paper.
- **The Homepage importer.** Paste your Homepage `services.yaml`, `docker.yaml` and `settings.yaml`, see the plan, and apply it: a board, the integrations, and your secrets moved into Cha Dash's encrypted vault. Run it again and nothing changes twice.
- **Onboarding.** First run goes setup token, owner account, then an offer to add a passkey. **Connect your home** then lets you import, discover (only the machines and Docker endpoints you name; it never scans a network), or add services one by one.
- **Least-privilege recipes.** Every integration's form shows how to make a credential that can only read, with commands to copy and a `pveum` generator for Proxmox. **Test connection** reads back what the credential can actually do, and says so if it could write.
- **Passkeys.** Sign in with a passkey, no username needed, and manage them under Settings → Security. They need HTTPS (or `localhost`) and `CHADASH_PUBLIC_URL`.
- **Export and import.** Settings → Backup, or `chadash export` and `chadash import`: your boards, integrations and settings as one YAML file, with secrets as placeholders or sealed with a passphrase. Importing shows a field-by-field diff first, and never deletes anything.
- **A first paint with real values.** The page arrives with your last-known state already in it, then stays live over a single WebSocket.
- **An installable app that works offline.** Install it to your home screen (over HTTPS), and it shows the last-known state when the hub can't be reached.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA is a release requirement: automated axe checks cover every view and every widget state, and every theme's text meets 4.5:1 contrast.
- **Demo mode.** The Demo Home simulator, a made-up homelab with scripted incidents, for trying Cha Dash without connecting anything (`compose.demo.yaml`).
- **Ways to run it.** Docker Compose, with zero required settings and a hardened container (non-root, read-only filesystem, no capabilities) next to an optional read-only Docker socket proxy. Images for linux/amd64 and linux/arm64, plus linux/arm/v7 as best-effort, signed with cosign and carrying build provenance and an SBOM. A Community Applications template for Unraid. Static binaries for Linux, and macOS builds for development.
- **Light to run.** One self-contained binary with the web app inside, and gzip compression for the app, the first paint and the API: the Demo Home's first page goes from 145 kB to 24 kB.
